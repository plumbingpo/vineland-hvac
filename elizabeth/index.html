How Elizabeth Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Many mid-season breakdowns trace back to skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Swap your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil every spring. Then book a professional tune-up each spring and fall, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Take a look at the age of your system. That number says a lot about what comes next. Most well-maintained air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that range and is also showing higher bills, uneven temperatures, or frequent repairs, it's a strong candidate for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than an emergency replacement when the first heat wave hits.

Small problems don't stay minor when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and spares you a few miserable nights.
